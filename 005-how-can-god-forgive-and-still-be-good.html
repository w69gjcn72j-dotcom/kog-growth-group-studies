How Can God Forgive and Still Be Good?

Romans 3:21–31 · August 2026
Big IdeaSince all have sinned, all are justified by faith in Jesus Christ, whom God presented as an atoning sacrifice to show his righteousness.

Aim

(1) See why God cannot simply let sin go; (2) rest in the finished work of Christ rather than in our own record; (3) name one household to pray for and invite.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • Think of a time someone owed you money, dented your car, or let you down at work. What did you actually want — an apology, repayment, or for it to be quietly forgotten? Why?
  • Where do you notice yourself keeping a mental scoreboard — at work, with the children, at church morning tea? What does that scoreboard do to you?

Read — Romans 3:21-31 (CSB)

21 But now, apart from the law, the righteousness of God has been revealed, attested by the Law and the Prophets. 22 The righteousness of God is through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe, since there is no distinction. 23 For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; 24 they are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

25 God presented him as an atoning sacrifice in his blood, received through faith, to demonstrate his righteousness, because in his restraint God passed over the sins previously committed. 26 God presented him to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time, so that he would be just and justify the one who has faith in Jesus.

27 Where, then, is boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? By one of works? No, on the contrary, by a law of faith. 28 For we conclude that a person is justified by faith apart from the works of the law. 29 Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles too? Yes, of Gentiles too, 30 since there is one God who will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith. 31 Do we then nullify the law through faith? Absolutely not! On the contrary, we uphold the law.

Context

Paul has spent two and a half chapters showing that nobody is righteous — not the outsider without the law, not the religious insider with it. Everyone stands guilty. Then comes the great “But now” of verse 21: a righteousness from God, not earned by law-keeping but received by faith in Jesus. Two words carry the weight — redemption (a price paid to set someone free) and atoning sacrifice (Jesus bearing God’s just anger in our place). This is not God going soft. It is God being just and the one who justifies.

Discuss

  1. Read verses 21-22. What is the significance of the words “But now”? What has changed, and what has not?
  2. Verse 23 says there is no distinction — all have sinned. Which sins have you quietly renamed so they no longer feel like sin? (“I’m just being honest”, “righteous anger”, “sharing a prayer point”.)
  3. Verse 24 says we are justified freely by grace through redemption. If it is free to us, what did it cost God?
  4. Read verses 25-26 slowly. Why would it be unrighteous of God simply to forgive us and say nothing more about it?
  5. David said forgiveness is more than letting someone off the hook. How does the cross show that God takes our sin more seriously than we do, and loves us more than we dare hope?
  6. Verses 27-28 exclude all boasting. Where does boasting sneak back into Christian life — how long we have been at church, how we parent, how we serve?
  7. Verses 29-31: how does justification by faith level the ground between people we would naturally rank differently — at work, among neighbours, across our two congregations?
  8. What would change this week at your workplace or dinner table if you truly believed your standing before God is settled and cannot be improved by your performance?

  • Thank God that he is both just and the one who justifies — he did not lower the standard, he paid the price.

Leader’s Notes

Your job tonight is to point joyfully to Jesus, not to extract confessions. Some in the room may be guests, or not yet Christians, or carrying real guilt. Never corner anyone — answer questions in the first person before you ask them of others.

Brief answers to key questions

  • Q1: “But now” marks the turn from verdict to rescue. What has not changed is our guilt; what has changed is that God has revealed a righteousness apart from the law, attested by the Law and the Prophets — so it does not contradict God’s justice.
  • Q3: Free to us, costly to God. Redemption is a purchase price — God in Christ paid what we could never pay.
  • Q4: If God simply overlooked sin he would be unjust, treating evil as though it did not matter. In his restraint he passed over former sins; at the cross he dealt with them.
  • Q6: Boasting returns as comparison. The law of faith excludes it, because faith is empty hands receiving a gift, not a hand holding out a resume.
