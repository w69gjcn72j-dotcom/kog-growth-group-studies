The Sinner in the Mirror

Romans 3:9–20 · July 2026
Big IdeaThe law of God exposes our true, utterly sinful selves — all alike under sin’s power, not even one of us righteous — so that every mouth is stopped before the judgement of God.

Aim

  • See that no one is righteous — including us — so we stop grading ourselves on a curve against colleagues, neighbours and other parents.
  • Let God’s law expose the respectable, renamed sins of our tongues and hearts.
  • Respond with a healthy fear of God that drives us to our knees, to Jesus, and to prayer for the lost.

You’ll need: Bibles (CSB), pens, about 60–75 minutes.

Connect (10 min)

  • When did you last catch sight of yourself unexpectedly — a shop window, a photo someone else took? What made it confronting?
  • At work or the school gate we quietly compare: ’At least I’m not like that colleague / that driver / that parent.’ Why is comparing ourselves to others so comforting — and so misleading?

Read — Romans 3:9–20 (CSB)

9 What then? Are we any better off? Not at all! For we have already charged that both Jews and Greeks are all under sin, 10 as it is written: There is no one righteous, not even one. 11 There is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God. 12 All have turned away; all alike have become worthless. There is no one who does what is good, not even one.

13 Their throat is an open grave; they deceive with their tongues. Vipers’ venom is under their lips. 14 Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. 15 Their feet are swift to shed blood; 16 ruin and wretchedness are in their paths, 17 and the path of peace they have not known. 18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.

19 Now we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those who are subject to the law, so that every mouth may be shut and the whole world may become subject to God’s judgment. 20 For no one will be justified in his sight by the works of the law, because the knowledge of sin comes through the law.

Context

Paul has spent Romans 1–2 proving that Gentiles without the law and Jews with the law both stand guilty before God. Verses 9–20 are his closing address: a chain of Old Testament quotations (mostly Psalms) that leaves nobody in the courtroom with anything to say. The “law” in vv.19–20 means the whole Old Testament — and its God-given job is not to save us but to show us our sin, like a mirror with no filters.

Discuss

  1. In vv.10–12, how many times does Paul say ’no one’ or ’not even one’? What exactly does he say no one is or does?
  2. Verses 13–18 tour the body — throat, tongue, lips, mouth, feet, eyes. Why do you think the list ends with ’no fear of God before their eyes’ (v.18)?
  3. V.9 says all alike are ’under sin’ — sin as a slave-master, not occasional mistakes (John 8:34). How does that differ from ’a good person deep down’?
  4. At the centre of vv.10–12 sits ’no one seeks God.’ Why does God measure righteousness by our relationship with him rather than by comparison with others?
  5. According to vv.19–20, what is the law’s God-given job — and what can it never do? Why does that silence every mouth?
  6. We rename our sins to keep them respectable: gossip becomes ’I’m just being honest’, bitterness becomes ’righteous anger’, a juicy story becomes ’sharing a prayer point’. Which renamed sin is your tongue most tempted by — at work, at home, or over morning tea?
  7. If no one seeks God on their own, then every believer — and every newcomer — is a miracle of God’s grace. How would believing that change how we welcome new people as our 9:15 English and Chinese congregations combine?
  8. Fight, flight, freeze or fawn — the right response to the fear of God is to fall on our knees. What would it look like this week for your mouth to go silent before God so you can listen (v.19; James 1:22–25)?

Live It Out

  • KOG 3·2·1·0 — Pray for 3 by name (50,000 households sit within 10 minutes of us, and none will seek God unless he moves — join Tuesday prayer). Connect with 2 — a neighbour, colleague or school-gate parent. Invite 1 — to church, Welcome to Life, or a dad to Focus on Dads before Father’s Day (6/9). Leave 0 unloved.
  • Weekly challenge: each day read the Bible, then sit in two minutes of silence — the mirror with no filter. Before speaking or posting in any group chat, ask: cursing and bitterness, or the path of peace?
  • Memory verse: “There is no one righteous, not even one.” — Romans 3:10 (CSB)

Pray

  • Confess together, using vv.10–18 as your mirror — and thank the Lord Jesus, who faced God’s judgement in our place so our silence could become praise.
  • Pray by name for the 3 people each member is praying for — that the God who alone makes seekers would open their hearts.
  • Pray for the combining 9:15 congregations, the new midweek fellowships and Walk with Me — that St Paul’s would visibly walk the path of peace before the households around us.

Leader’s Notes

  • Set the tone: joyfully point to Jesus. Guests and not-yet-Christians may be present, and this passage indicts all of us equally — never corner anyone. Go first in honest confession.
  • Q1: ’no one / not even one’ comes six times; vv.10–12 form a chiasm centred on ’no one seeks God’ — righteousness is relational. Q2: the eyes come last because ’no fear of God’ is the root diagnosis behind every symptom.
  • Q5: the law is ’the straight-edge that shows us how crooked we are’ — knowledge of sin, never justification. Let the bad news land; it sets up next Sunday’s ’But now’ (Romans 3:21–31).
  • Vision tie: this is the dark backdrop of ’from faith to faith’ (Rom 1:17). We’re at 50 of 75 households serving on the way to 500 — reached not by recruiting the righteous but by welcoming miracles.
  • Live initiatives: three new midweek fellowships launch this quarter (2 Chinese, 1 English) — home groups are the engine; Focus on Dads is praying for five new regular dads by Father’s Day (6/9); consider pairing members through Walk with Me (121 discipleship).
